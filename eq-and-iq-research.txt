#Research EQ and IQ

###What is Emotional Intellegence?

Emotional Intelligence (EQ) is the ability to recognize, understand, and manage our own emotions while empathizing with the emotions and perspectives
of others to guide our actions. High or low EQ depends on our level of self-awareness, self-regulation, situational awareness, and empathy.

For example, if my friend 'Billy' makes a cup of tea by pouring milk before the hot water, a low-EQ response would be to immediately criticize him,
imposing a "my way is right" mindset that ignores his background and habits. A high-EQ response involves exercising self-regulation to pause 
the impulse to judge - showing curiosity about his approach and recognizing that there are many valid ways to reach the shared goal of 
making a cup of tea.

###How is EQ differnet from IQ?

While IQ measures cognitive ability, logical reasoning, and technical problem-solving, EQ measures how effectively we understand, manage, and 
navigate our emotions and relationships.

Using the cup of tea analogy, an "IQ lens" focuses on procedural knowledge and execution - knowing that water must reach boiling point, 
understanding the function of tea bags and following logical steps to prepare the cup of tea. In contrast, an "EQ lens" focuses on the 
interpersonal environment - showing awareness of how people prefer their tea, managing your reaction if someone brews it differently and 
maintaining a positive connection while achieving the goal. In short, IQ helps you solve the problem, while EQ helps you manage the people 
and context surrounding it.


###Why does EQ matter?

I've always thought of it this way,: EQ is essential because technical knowledge and strategy (IQ) can only take a team so far; it is the human 
element that determines how effectively a team executes its goals. While technical skills can be learned through training, EQ provides the 
foundational environment for those skills to thrive.

The most effective leaders recognize that team members are not just task-executors, but individuals driven by distinct motivations, perspectives 
and emotional needs. By consistently practicing self-awareness and empathy, leaders nurture an environment of trust and mutual respect. 
Ultimately, EQ matters because it transforms individual talent into collective strength - fostering resilience, safe conflict resolution
and sustainable success.
